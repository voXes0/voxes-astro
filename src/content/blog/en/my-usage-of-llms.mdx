<Lead>AI is the talk of everyone nowadays, so much so that it starts to feel annoying. But it is hard to ignore.</Lead>

Do I use AI? The short answer is yes; the longer answer is also yes, but a bit more complicated.

## My first approach

When I began to learn to code, I learnt it the somewhat traditional way, through online sources such as videos, books and documentation. I didn't like using any sort of LLMs, because during those times
they were often wrong and unreliable. It was only two years into my career that I used an LLM, ChatGPT in this case, to help me port [Kotsoft's Particle-based Viscoelastic Fluid Simulation](https://github.com/kotsoft/particle_based_viscoelastic_fluid)
into C#, the language I was learning and the one I was most confident in. I had little experience with JavaScript then.

### Incorporating it to my workflow

Since then, I've used it more often. It didn't do a very good job back then. it was buggy and unreliable, but LLMs as a whole have improved a lot since.
I faced some problems when I further experimented with them. They often wrote difficult to read, unorganized, buggy messes of code, and relying on them
limited how much I could innovate and improve upon my code. Since then, I've settled on exactly where and how they can be used for a positive impact
on a project, which I explain below.

## How I utilize them now

Today I mostly just use [Claude](https://claude.ai), for the following purposes:

### Planning and researching

One thing that really speeds up the development process is being able to research a practical topic or problem and get a helpful answer immediately. That answer
can even be used to do more precise research on the topic on the internet. The other is having a template to work upon for a particular project, such as a project scaffold or boilerplate, so you can quickly get to work.

### Designing and visualizing

On the frontend side, I find working with CSS the traditional way too time-consuming and, frankly, boring. More often than not, I use Claude for the CSS and elements.
I make the color palette and theme, lay the foundations, give the ideas, and describe the kind of design I would like to experiment with, and through multiple iterations and many, many tweaks, get the final page done.
In short, I direct the design and Claude executes it. It is also sometimes used for helping with JavaScript components such as the homepage animation sequence of this website.

### Debugging and general optimization

Sometimes finding those bugs becomes a real nuisance. Claude comes in handy by writing tests in bulk.
It is also useful for writing benchmarks, which I run myself, to find performance bottlenecks, and for proposing somewhat surface-level optimizations for simpler parts of the code.

### Code cleanup, documentation, and minor questions

They can really help with skipping the most boring parts of a project. I use Claude for cleaning the codebase and documenting it for future me or others. Everything is still human-reviewed, of course. I read every diff, and necessary adjustments are made to the documentation.
The other usage is finding the answer to a specific minor problem quickly. for example, I learnt Git and Linux commands by asking: How do I do x with Git/Linux?

## Where I *don't* use them

Whenever the code is for performance or security critical applications, or when innovation is required, everything is hand-written. The same goes for anywhere else where reliability and correctness are crucial and a bug cannot be afforded.
Claude may then be used for testing and benchmarking them. Examples of this are most of the [Vofluid](https://voxes.ir/works/vofluid) project's core logic and most of the backend of the [Kazheh](https://voxes.ir/works/kazheh) project.
